## The shape of the answer

The easiest way to remember:

```
NOW  →  BEFORE  →  NEXT ⭐

1. NOW    — who I am today   (role + years + stack + current project)
2. BEFORE — where I came from (2–3 projects, quickly)
3. NEXT   — what I want now   (and why THIS company)
```

```
   NOW              BEFORE                    NEXT
   ────             ──────                    ────
"I'm Priyanka,   "Before RoboGebra I      "Now I'm looking for
 5 years,         worked on Subsea         ______ , which is why
 Angular +        and EasyVisa…"           I'm interested in
 Java…"                                    [COMPANY]."
   ↑ 20 sec          ↑ 25 sec                  ↑ 15 sec
```

⭐ **Why this order works:** the interviewer decides in the first ten seconds whether you sound senior. Give them role + years + stack **immediately**, then earn it with the projects.

Real-world idea: a **film trailer**. The title and the star come first, then the story, then "in cinemas soon." Nobody starts a trailer with the production history.

#### Easy memory

```
NOW → BEFORE → NEXT ⭐
20 sec | 25 sec | 15 sec  = 60 seconds ⭐
First sentence = ROLE + YEARS + STACK ⭐ (they judge you in 10 seconds)
```

## How to say it

### The five rules

```
1. FIRST SENTENCE = role + years + stack ⭐
      "Full Stack Developer, 5 years, Angular and Java with Spring Boot."
      → they judge your level in 10 seconds. Don't make them wait.

2. Say "I BUILT", not "I was involved in" ⭐
      ❌ "I helped with the quiz module"
      ✅ "I built the quiz module"

3. Name the MODULE, not the technology ⭐
      ❌ "I used Angular and Java"
      ✅ "I built the Study List module with real-time sync"

4. ONE flagship, the rest in a line each ⭐
      Spend half your words on RoboGebra. Don't give four projects
      equal time — it flattens the story.

5. PAUSE after the first sentence ⭐
      Let the headline land. Then continue.
```

### Speaking tips

```
🗣️ Speak SLOWER than feels natural — nerves speed you up by ~20%
🗣️ Short sentences. Full stops. This is speech, not an essay ⭐
🗣️ Smile before you start — it changes your voice, even on a call
🗣️ Don't recite the resume line by line — they already have it ⭐
🗣️ If you go past 75 seconds on the 60-second version, you're over-explaining
```

### ⚠️ Three things NOT to say

```
❌ "I'm a fresher-level…" or "I only worked on small parts…"
      → you have 5 years and four real projects. Own them ⭐

❌ "RoboGebra uses MySQL"
      → it's MONGODB ⭐ (this was wrong in the old notes)

❌ Naming a technology you can't discuss for two minutes
      → if you say NgRx, be ready for "how do effects work?"
        Say what you've USED, and be honest about the rest ⭐
```

## The follow-ups that always come next

Your intro **plants** these. Have a 30-second answer ready for each.

### 1. "Tell me more about RoboGebra." ⭐ (almost guaranteed)

> "It's an AI-based maths learning platform. A student picks an exercise, and the platform gives a step-by-step solution — in Tamil or English — with the reasoning and formula breakdown, not just the answer. The backend is Java 17 and Spring Boot 3 on MongoDB, and there are three front ends: an Angular web app, an Ionic mobile app I built for iOS and Android, and an admin portal."

### 2. "Which part are you proudest of?" ⭐

> "The Ionic mobile app. We had a working Angular web app, and the ask was to reach mobile without rebuilding. I built it with Ionic and Capacitor, reusing our existing services and models, and added about nineteen native plugins — push notifications through Firebase, native Razorpay checkout, offline detection. The genuinely new part for me was the **Ionic page lifecycle**: Ionic keeps pages alive in the navigation stack, so `ngOnInit` runs only once and any data that must be fresh has to be loaded in `ionViewWillEnter`."

⭐ That last sentence is a real technical detail. It turns a project story into evidence.

### 3. "What was the hardest problem you solved?" ⭐

> "An N+1 query problem. We were loading a chapter's exercises and then fetching each exercise's items one at a time — one query plus N. I replaced it with a single MongoDB aggregation pipeline that matches on all the exercise ids at once and sorts them, so it became one round trip instead of fifty. What I took from it is that switching to a document database doesn't remove the N+1 problem — it just changes what it looks like."

### 4. "Why are you looking to change?" ⭐

Keep it **forward-looking**. Never criticise your current employer.

> "I've had good ownership at Provility — I've built features end to end across four products. What I want next is more scale: a larger user base, a bigger engineering team to learn from, and more structured processes around code review and architecture. That's what interests me about [COMPANY]."

```
✅ What you want MORE of ⭐
❌ What you're escaping FROM 💥
```

### 5. "Why this company?"

⚠️ **Prepare this per company.** A generic answer is worse than a short one.

```
Name ONE specific thing:
   their product, their domain, the scale, the role's stack,
   or something in the JD you genuinely want to work on ⭐
```
