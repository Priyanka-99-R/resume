# Self-Introduction — "Tell Me About Yourself"

This is the very first thing most interviewers ask. Nail it, and you set a confident tone for the rest of the interview. Below are three versions (60s, 30s, 2-min), a cheat sheet of anchor facts, and delivery tips. Practice out loud until it feels natural — not memorized word-for-word, but smooth.

---

## 60-Second Version (Primary — memorize this one)

> "Hi, I'm Priyanka, a Full Stack Developer with over four years of experience building web applications, primarily on the Angular front end and Java with Spring Boot on the back end.
>
> I currently work as a Software Engineer at Provility Software Solutions in Chennai, where I've delivered across several products. My most recent and favourite project is RoboGebra — an AI-driven math learning platform built with Angular, Ionic, Java and MySQL. There I built a personalized learning dashboard, a study reminder system backed by REST APIs, a quiz module, and I contributed to an AI math explanation engine that gives bilingual explanations in Tamil and English, along with real-time math computation and visualization.
>
> Before that, I worked on Subsea — a marine operations management product using Angular and .NET on Azure — and on EasyVisa, a visa management platform for attorneys, where I built a document portal and reusable Angular Formly components with nested reactive forms. I've also led an Angular version migration, upgrading four repositories to Angular 16.
>
> On the technical side I'm strong in JavaScript, TypeScript, RxJS, NgRx and Redux on the front end, and Spring Boot, microservices and SQL databases like MySQL and Postgres on the back end. I enjoy owning a feature end to end — from API to UI.
>
> I'm now looking for a role with more scale and variety, which is exactly why I'm excited about Wipro."

## Tone & Delivery Tips

- **Lead with the headline.** First sentence = role + years + core stack. The interviewer decides in 10 seconds whether you sound senior; give them the answer immediately.
- **Present → past → future.** Current role first, then a quick arc of past projects, then close with why you're here (Wipro). This structure feels intentional, not rambling.
- **Pick ONE flagship.** Spend the most words on RoboGebra. Mention others briefly. Don't list every project equally — it flattens your story.
- **Talk in outcomes, not just tech.** "I built a quiz module and a bilingual explanation engine" beats "I used Angular and Java."
- **Don't recite your resume line by line.** They have it. Give the narrative version.
- **End with intent.** Always close on why you're interviewing — it bridges naturally into "Why Wipro?"
- **Pace and pause.** Speak slightly slower than feels natural. Pause after your headline sentence so it lands.
- **Confidence over modesty.** Use "I built / I led / I delivered," not "I helped with" or "I was involved in."
- **Keep it tight.** If you're past 75 seconds on the 60s version, you're over-explaining. Practice with a timer.
- **Smile and breathe.** This is the one answer you can fully prepare — let that calm carry you into the harder questions.
