# HR & Behavioral Interview — Questions & Sample Answers

These are the most common HR and managerial-round questions for an experienced (lateral) candidate, with sample answers written in first person and tailored to Priyanka's background. Use the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) where the question is about a real scenario. Adapt the wording so it sounds like you — don't recite robotically.

---

## 1. Tell me about yourself.

**Sample answer:**

I specialize in Angular on the front end and Java with Spring Boot on the back end. My most recent project is RoboGebra, an AI-driven math learning platform where I built the learning dashboard, a quiz module, and a study reminder system using REST APIs. I've also worked across .NET, microservices, and cloud on AWS and Azure. I'm now looking for a role with more scale and variety, which is what draws me to Wipro."

## 6. What is your biggest achievement?

**Sample answer (STAR):**
"**Situation:** On RoboGebra, one of the key differentiators was giving students math explanations in their own language. **Task:** I was responsible for building the front-end of the AI math explanation feature and the surrounding learning experience — dashboard, quiz, and reminders. **Action:** I built the personalized learning dashboard and quiz module in Angular and Ionic, integrated the AI explanation engine to deliver bilingual Tamil and English explanations, and wired up real-time math computation and visualization. I also built a study reminder system backed by REST APIs so students stayed on track. **Result:** The platform delivered a genuinely personalized, accessible learning experience, especially for Tamil-speaking students who could finally learn math in their native language. It's the project I'm most proud of because it combined clean engineering with real user impact."

## 7. Tell me about a time you had a conflict with a teammate.

**Sample answer (STAR):**
"**Situation:** On one project, a backend teammate and I disagreed on the API contract for a form-heavy module — they wanted one large endpoint, I wanted more granular ones for the nested reactive forms I was building. **Task:** We needed to agree quickly without blocking the sprint. **Action:** Instead of pushing my view, I set up a short call, walked through the actual UI flows and how the form state would be saved, and listened to their concern about round-trips and performance. We landed on a middle ground — a couple of focused endpoints plus one batch save. **Result:** It unblocked both of us within a day, the integration was clean, and we actually used that same pattern on later modules. I learned that most 'conflicts' are just two valid priorities that haven't been talked through yet."

## 8. How do you handle tight deadlines?

**Sample answer:**
"I break the work down first and figure out what's truly must-have versus nice-to-have, then communicate early if something is at risk — surprises are worse than bad news. A real example: during the Subsea project I had bug fixes plus the Schedule-Manager module to deliver in a short window. I prioritized the blocking bugs, got the core Schedule-Manager flow with the Kendo UI grid working end to end first, and left polish for last. I also kept my lead updated daily so expectations were aligned. We shipped on time. Under pressure I stay calm, prioritize ruthlessly, and over-communicate."

## 9. Tell me about a time you failed, and what you learned.

**Sample answer (STAR):**
"**Situation:** Early in my career on EasyVisa, I built a reusable Formly component and assumed my local testing was enough, so I didn't write thorough unit tests for an edge case. **Task:** The component was meant to handle dynamic nested forms. **Action:** A configuration I hadn't tested broke in another part of the app during QA. I owned it immediately, fixed it, and then went back and added proper unit tests covering the edge cases. **Result:** It was caught before release, but it taught me to treat unit tests as part of 'done,' not an afterthought. Since then, writing tests for the components I own has been a standard part of my workflow, and it's saved me many times."

## 10. How do you handle feedback or criticism?

When I do disagree after that, I explain my view with context and we decide together. Feedback is faster learning, so I'd rather hear it early than discover a problem in production."

## Quick Reminders for the HR Round

- **Stay positive about your current employer** — never badmouth Provility or teammates. Frame your move as growth, not escape.
- **Use real project names** — RoboGebra, Subsea, EasyVisa, the Angular 16 migration. Specifics make you credible.
- **Quantify or describe impact** where you can, even qualitatively ("reused across the team," "delivered on time," "all four repos migrated").
- **Keep answers 30-90 seconds.** Long enough to show substance, short enough to keep attention.
- **Be honest** about notice period, relocation, and salary — consistency builds trust.
- **Smile, make eye contact, and pause before answering** tough questions. Composure reads as seniority.
